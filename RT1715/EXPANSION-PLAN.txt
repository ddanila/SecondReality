Robotron: additional Second Reality chapters
===========================================
Recorded 2026-10-16 on rt1715.
Status: implemented as optional expanded/extended cuts and runnable studies.
See EXPANSION.txt for commands, editorial decisions and implementation limits.

Purpose
-------
Expand the presentation from an exterior tour and technical credits into a
journey through the machine. Reinterpret original Second Reality effects using
Robotron geometry, boards, markings and documented functional relationships.
Every effect discussed is recorded below. The first release uses the strongest
three additions; later stages evaluate the remaining effects as complete scenes
or short transitions. Inclusion depends on visual quality and narrative fit.

This is a DOS PC presentation about the Robotron. Effects displayed through its
CRT are artistic transitions, not evidence that a U880 could render them. Actual
boot text continues to come from the documented DAC reference capture.

Baseline and constraints
------------------------
- Preserve the current short version as a selectable cut and regression target.
- Keep the original drone and the existing scenic flight, disk insertion,
  latch action, boot, wide reveal, near-camera swoosh and technical credits.
- Preserve the flight's local MUSIC0 cues: overview 24:54, swoosh 25:6,
  flight end 26:57; credits start 28:0 and end 43:29.
- Keep all 21 credit slots and their reading time in the short and first expanded
  cuts. A later longer cut may relocate the credits but must retain their length
  and deliberately remap and validate every musical entry.
- Build, convert and precalculate on the host. Test DOSBox-X headlessly, with
  unattended completion and no confirmation windows.
- Start from the current dynamic486, 32 MiB, 300000-cycle profile. Record any
  justified change; visual quality and reliable pacing take precedence over
  marginal speed gains. Do not assume an effect fits until measured.
- Resolve and hash the latest source model at implementation time. Keep sibling
  repositories read-only; retain reproducible source identities for each build.
- Extend the existing claim index for new factual labels. Distinguish specimen
  observations, reference configurations and illustrative signal diagrams.
- Keep text legible at the actual DOS resolution and 4:3 display aspect. Prefer
  short labels, stable reading holds and restrained green/amber accents.
- Do not add personal/model-author credits to the on-screen presentation;
  preserve required provenance and notices in the companion documentation.

Narrative and release structure
-------------------------------
First expanded cut, playback order:
  Short identity -> particle-built mark -> board lens/rotozoom inspection ->
  data tunnel -> existing close flight and whole-computer reveal -> credits.

Opening material shows components and abstract forms, avoiding a full exterior
view that would spoil the flight's reveal. The identity and particle sections
may be one scene. Duration estimates below are storyboard budgets, not approved
music cues; musical phrases determine final lengths.

Later extended cut:
  The same opening and flight -> deliberate transition through the CRT ->
  internal assembly/transparency chapter and selected abstract effects ->
  return to the machine -> complete technical credits.

The later extension cannot simply occupy the existing flight-to-credit gap.
That gap already has musical duties. Establish a new musical route and credit
cue mapping before integrating additional scenes there. Keep the first expanded
cut available even if this longer structure is adopted.

Implementation order
--------------------
0. Baseline, source audit and music storyboard.
1. Lens/rotozoomer board inspection: first standalone visual prototype.
2. Dot tunnel: build the connection from inspection to the existing flight.

Implementation order differs from playback order: validate the most useful new
scene first. Build each effect as a separately previewable chapter before adding
it to the complete sequence. Do not silently replace the short-cut defaults.

Confirm each candidate against executable mappings and source implementation.

entry (RT1715/SEQUENCE.INC). Adding earlier effects requires either restoring
selected MUSIC1 passages and their module handoff or explicitly retiming the
remakes to another reviewed musical arrangement. Do not concatenate arbitrary
time slices or assume original frame counts still match a new scene.

Tasks:

- Inspect MUSIC1 order/row timing, effect-entry markers, original module switch

- Produce a cue sheet: cut, scene, module, entry/exit order:row, duration,

Stage 1: board inspection with lens and rotozoomer
------------------------------------------------
Sources: LENS/MAIN.C, LENS/ASM.ASM, LENS/MAKEFILE and associated asset generators.
Original executable: LNS&ZOOM.EXE. Relative effort: medium.
Provisional duration: 15-25 seconds.

Use a high-quality render of a real source board. Reveal the board, move a lens
to the U880 and selected RAM/controller components, pause on readable markings,
then rotate/zoom into the circuitry. Choose a coherent board or explicitly
transition between boards; never imply unrelated components share a board.

Reuse image sampling, lens lookup logic and motion concepts. Generate new
backgrounds, palettes, focus coordinates and lens paths on the host. Reuse the
component selection and evidence already available to the credit tooling.
Avoid a tiny source bitmap enlarged beyond useful detail: prepare sufficient
source resolution or discrete detail levels with stable transitions.

Acceptance: source-correct chip focus; readable holds; no lens edge corruption,
palette flashes, objectionable aliasing or unintended image repetition. A
single-scene host preview and headless DOS capture must agree on composition.

Stage 2: dot tunnel / journey through data
----------------------------------------
Sources: TUNNELI/TUN10.PAS, ROUTINES.ASM and generated tunnel tables.
Original executable: TUNNELI.EXE. Relative effort: medium.
Provisional duration: 6-12 seconds.

Transform the board inspection into an abstract tunnel inspired by connector
pins and traces. Suggest keyboard -> CPU -> memory -> display through changes
in shape and restrained pulses, with minimal text. This is functional imagery,
not a traced bus transaction or a claim about exact electrical timing.

Reuse projection/table ideas, replacing steering and ring profiles. Host-build
any necessary conversion or port; do not introduce guest Pascal compilation.
End on a composition/colour that hands naturally to the flight's opening.

Acceptance: continuous movement and speed, no abrupt camera flips or clipping,
no dense flickering dots, a clearly planned musical exit into the flight.

Stage 3: particle identity and cinematic introduction
----------------------------------------------------
Particle sources: DOTS/MAIN.C, ASM.ASM, FACE.C and MAKEFILE.
Original executable: MINVBALL.EXE. Relative effort: medium.
Opening references: ALKU and original launcher/script introduction sequence.
Provisional combined duration: 8-15 seconds.

Particles gather into a recognizable Robotron mark or 1715M lettering, hold,
then resolve into a keyboard matrix or board geometry used by the lens scene.
Reuse projection/gravity/bounce routines where appropriate; target-shape
sampling and controlled morphing are new work. Avoid morph paths that create
an unreadable cloud or merely substitute one static logo for another.

Borrow the original introduction's typography, framing and deliberate pacing
for a brief identity/title treatment. Do not reveal the complete exterior yet.
The full spaceship/explosion introduction is a lower-priority alternative;
prototype only if the expanded musical structure warrants its added length.

Acceptance: recognizable title at native size, intentional settling and exit,
no long blank opening or gratuitous white flash. Assemble and validate expanded
cut 1 with the unchanged flight and full-duration credits.

Stage 4: glenz transparency and internal assembly
------------------------------------------------
Sources: GLENZ/MAIN.C, rendering assembly and MAKEFILE.
Original executable: GLENZ.EXE. Relative effort: high.
Provisional duration: 12-20 seconds in the later extended cut.

After the whole-machine reveal, show a simplified translucent case surrounding
opaque source-model boards and drives. Separate assemblies slightly, rotate to
expose their relationships, and reassemble. Reuse source transforms and the
credit cutaway's component grouping. Exploded positions are illustrative;
they are not a mechanical disassembly procedure.

First prototype a few transparent shell surfaces around opaque parts. Compare
the original palette-based glenz approach with a limited transparency pass in
our protected-mode renderer. Resolve overlap ordering and depth behaviour
before committing to detailed geometry. Full-model transparency is not required
and must not turn chip leads and case seams into visual noise.

Acceptance: correct occlusion, stable overlaps, recognizable internal layout,
no false missing panels, readable motion and settled inspection views. Publish
the renderer tradeoff and measured playback cost with the prototype.

Stage 5: techno and plasma accents
----------------------------------
Techno sources: TECHNO/KOE.C and associated assembly; TECHNO.EXE.
Relative effort: medium. Provisional duration: 4-8 seconds.
Adapt interference/scanning patterns and music-row pulses into an abstract
machine-waking passage. Resolve into a green display or a real boot view.
Keep artistic effects distinct from captured boot output; avoid adding fake
OS messages. Gate: rhythm supports the transition without excessive flashing
or obscuring the machine's authentic display details.

Plasma sources: PLZPART/MAIN.C, PLZ.C, VECT.C and assembly; PLZPART.EXE.
Relative effort: medium-high. Provisional duration: 5-10 seconds.
Prototype a restrained signal field that becomes a component-shaped object or
fills the CRT during a transition. Start with original plasma behaviour, then
assess the new object's surface mapping and silhouette. Gate: the passage has
a clear place in the story and a clean handoff; otherwise retain it as an
optional standalone homage rather than lengthening the main cut.

For either effect, the original IDEAS proposal to enter a monitor containing
effects and return to the vector world is a useful reference. Design screen
framing, projection, palette and musical continuity together; it is not a
simple insert into the current timed credit handoff.

Stage 6: remaining candidates and explicit decision criteria
-----------------------------------------------------------
Comanche / sinus landscape
  Sources: COMAN/MAIN.C, THELOOP machinery, MAKEFILE; 3DSINFLD.EXE.
  Idea: stylized PCB terrain, with traces as roads and chips as raised blocks.
  Effort: high for a useful adaptation. It is a height-field renderer, not a
  general renderer for our source models; overhangs/leads need another method.
  Prototype a small board-inspired height field. Include only if its composition
  adds a distinct experience beyond our existing close 3D flight. Label its
  simplified geometry as an artistic interpretation in companion documentation.

Mirror-ball / water scroller
  Sources: WATER/DEMO.PAS and its tables/generators; RAYSCRL.EXE.
  Idea: a short reflective badge or closing motif using Robotron artwork.
  Effort: medium-high; inspect precomputed reflection assets before promising
  arbitrary live reflections. Prototype one new motif and its host asset path.
  Include only if readable and visually connected to the machine; avoid another
  long technical scroller duplicating the existing credits.

Mountain scroller
  Sources: FOREST/READ2.PAS and associated assets; MNTSCRL.EXE.
  Idea: a short board-silhouette landscape carrying a title or chapter marker.
  Effort: medium-high, subject to the source asset-format audit. Prototype a
  single replacement landscape/title. Include if it provides a useful chapter
  transition without repeating the particle identity or PCB landscape scene.

Jelly picture
  Sources: JPLOGO source and build mapping; JPLOGO.EXE.
  Idea: a brief deforming Robotron badge/image that settles into crisp lettering.
  Effort: medium, pending asset-path verification. Prototype a source-derived
  badge and controlled settling. Include as a transition only if distortion
  remains intentional and the final identity is readable.

For each candidate, retain a preview and a written include/defer decision with
its reason. These are planned evaluations, not promises to insert every effect
into one long presentation. Avoid redundant scenes and preserve the best cut.

Shared implementation design
----------------------------
- Add a scene/cut manifest with explicit asset references, source identities,
  music cues and entry/exit contracts. Use it to drive previews and validation.
- Keep useful original algorithms/assembly, but adapt unsafe memory assumptions
  and host asset generation. Choose original real-mode execution versus a
  protected-mode port per effect after the dependency audit, not by default.
- Give each scene explicit ownership and cleanup of video mode, palette,
  interrupt hooks and buffers. Preserve the resident music/launcher contract.
- Provide standalone scene playback, deterministic capture and full-cut playback.
  A standalone preview must state when it substitutes a music entry or context.
- Tie motion to elapsed demo/music time, not raw rendered frame counts. Confirm
  behaviour when frames are skipped and at a second emulation speed.
- Precompute assets/tables on the host as appropriate. Keep live DOS rendering
  where it defines the effect; document any baked representation and footprint.
- Extend source/claim provenance, package manifests and checksums for every new
  asset. Verify original music preservation if modules remain unedited.

Validation and completion gates
-------------------------------
For each scene:
  Inspect a native-size still and a continuous capture; check focus, flicker,
  clipping, palette stability, speed and entry/exit. Add meaningful checks for
  asset formats, cue mapping and state cleanup rather than mirroring code.

For expanded cut 1:
  Verify musical handoff to MUSIC0 order 18, all existing flight-local events,
  every credit entry and full credit duration. Reuse existing flight references
  and scene-relative samples; do not mistake shifted wall time for a regression.

For extended cut 2:
  Review its new music structure end to end. Explicitly version the remapped
  credits cue sheet, preserve full reading duration and verify the return from
  the internal/CRT chapter. The original short cut must still pass its old cues.

For release:
  Run complete headless DOSBox-X playback, check audio/video transitions and
  clean exit, package selectable cuts and notices, then launch a fresh extracted
  package outside the development tree. Check every packaged hash and source
  companion. Record evidence and update editing/run documentation.
