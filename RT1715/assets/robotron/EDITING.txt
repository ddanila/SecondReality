Robotron presentation editing
=============================

One entry point: presentation.json. It selects the canonical source assembly,
flight.json, credits.json, materials.json, optional SCAD replacements, and
transition settings. All paths are relative to the file that contains them.

Validate before rendering:
  RT1715/build/host-python/bin/python RT1715/presentation.py

Build, run headlessly, and create the preview/review:
  RT1715/build/host-python/bin/python RT1715/build.py \
    --presentation RT1715/assets/robotron/presentation.json --review

Build tools and OpenSCAD run on the host. DOSBox-X only plays/tests the result.
The sibling repositories remain read-only. A local source snapshot includes
current uncommitted model edits; snapshot.json records their hashes. Generated
exports must match browser/model.json. Concurrent source changes during copying
are rejected. To reproduce a prepared snapshot, also pass:
  --robotron RT1715/build/model-snapshot

Models
------
The canonical source directory supplies the entire assembly. To override one
DOS exterior part, add an entry to presentation.json's models list:
  {"name":"lid", "scad":"models/my-lid.scad", "material":"paint"}

Coordinates are millimetres in the assembly frame: X right, Y back, Z up.
Keep the part's position in that frame. The geometry is not automatically
rescaled to fill the original part. Common names include base, lid, stand,
monitor, monitor-joint, bezel, screen, keyboard and deck. Prepared report.json
lists every mesh name. Front assemblies may be split into several src-* parts.

Use low OpenSCAD tessellation for runtime replacements. Each encoded mesh must
fit 500 polygons, 500 vertices (including the center), 500 normals and the
64 KiB object format. Errors identify unsupported geometry; reduce/split it.
The original drone slot 28 is protected. Credit renders can use detailed models.

Runtime keys use keyMeshes plus coreMesh/insertMesh from the canonical manifest.
keycaps.py samples exterior sections and the dished top, preserving skirt steps
and rounded bodies while omitting hidden cavities. It does not decimate thin
shells. Black/red caps have pale ink; function keys use a silver face and a
blue-grey rim as an opaque palette approximation of their clear shells. Ink is
projected onto the rendered dish. Strokes expand by 0.12 bitmap cells per side,
with a 1.15 mm maximum cell size and a shared depth offset for pale/dark ink. Distant keys use a rounded 14-face/16-vertex prefix; close views use the full
source-derived geometry. Generated names start with key-r-, which tells the
renderer to select this new prefix; old key- names retain their six-face layout.
Do not change the prefix counts without updating painter.c as well. Every variant must still fit the
500-vertex/normal/polygon object limits.

Motion and materials
--------------------
flight.json contains camera eye/target tracks, drone positions and bank settings.
The drone uses continuous-acceleration interpolation and smoothed banking capped
at 16 degrees. The steering block looks 0.45 seconds ahead for both nose heading
and bank, then fades anticipation from 42 to 45 seconds so the final swoosh keeps
its original heading. The miniature block sets 23% scale in the initial key-row
channel and eases back to full size from 8 to 16 seconds. The original mesh is
unchanged. The camera rides just above the key tops while the drone flies below
their rims; preserve clearance when moving these opening waypoints. The camera also uses continuous-
acceleration interpolation: avoid duplicate positions or stop flags in the close
flyover. The follow block blends the target toward the drone (weight 0.55),
then smoothly releases tracking from fade_start=30 to end=36 seconds for the
monitor pass. Keep the wide camera stationary from 45.5 seconds onward and the
straight final crossing around 47.76 seconds: these preserve the musical anchors.

The turntable block rotates every computer part around a shared world-space
pivot in millimetres. Positive degrees turn counterclockwise when seen from
above (+Z); the drone is excluded. The current 90-degree turn eases in after
the drone disappears, from 48.5 to 57.2 seconds, before the final fade. Moving
parts require regenerated animation and route-cache files; the host build does
both automatically.

Black runtime caps use the existing dark ramp with a restricted charcoal range
in lighting.h, lifting shadow detail without brightening other black surfaces.
The original drone receives a small view-dependent highlight boost, clamped to
each original material's ramp. Mesh, palette and flight transforms are unchanged.

materials.json controls the eight Robotron ramps. Their palette slots are fixed;
the original drone colors are untouched. The screen is a smooth-normal surface
with a broad quadratic view-dependent reflection (all 16 shades of a narrow dark-green ramp) and a low glow floor. It has no alpha
transparency or additional geometry over the bezel.

Pictures and ASCII text
-----------------------
credits.json has 21 cards. Each card has a unique image ID, printable ASCII lines,
and exactly one source:
  "view": {...}                  select and annotate canonical assembly parts
  "photo": "pictures/board.png"  use your own rendered image
  "render_model": "models/board.scad"  render a standalone SCAD model

Keep image IDs when changing their contents. A custom image/model replaces the
view field. Standalone models render with automatic framing on black; supplied
photos are fitted on a black canvas. Both get a white border. Use a black
background in supplied renders if the picture itself should have no backdrop.

Views support parts, board, angle, flip, focus_chips, focus_parts,
focus_component, highlight_chips, highlight_parts, margin, annotation and title.
Focused views show a whole-board/part locator by default; locator:false disables
it. Keyboard cards use only the narrow controller assembly. Avoid showing a
large generic IC when the subject is a specific memory bank or connector.

Text is limited to printable ASCII and 50 characters per line. The presentation
validator additionally rejects lines that need smaller than a 16-pixel font to
fit, so shorten a line instead of squeezing it. Three concise lines work best:
a subject, a verified specification, and what that part does. Evidence and
reference-vs-specimen distinctions are in SOURCES.txt; keep those qualifications.

Generated build/prepared/credits-contact.png shows all final picture/text cards.
Per-card previews are alongside it. build/robotron/credit-views.json records the
selected parts and crops. Render hashes avoid repeating unchanged SCAD renders.

Transitions and timing
----------------------
The last overview fades out over 0.8 seconds. The first card starts at 28:0 with
the complete workstation; following cards move inside. Entries snap to eight-row
tracker accents, with 0.84-second eased entrances and 0.36-second exits. Captions
remain stationary for at least three seconds. Cue timing uses the live tracker
and retrace interpolation, so disk reads do not accumulate schedule drift.
The final fade ends at the original cue 43:29. Do not change card count or the
58-second flight interval without deliberately reauthoring music synchronization.

build/robotron/credit-cues.json is the readable schedule. Playback automatically
checks every entry and the end cue. build/robotron-preview.mp4 is the full run;
build/robotron-review.mp4 and robotron-review.png cover the detail shots, reveal,
flyby, credit handoff and representative technical cards.

Front-panel lettering is generated by RT1715/front_markings.py, in millimetres.
It is geometry in both the DOS scene and workstation credit photographs.
The model-name badge is authored above the drive openings; control labels use
canonical front_labels() positions. Keep runtime names prefixed ink- so their
small depth bias prevents interference with the fascia.

flight.json climb_framing: start/full/end control the smooth camera-distance
correction during drone scale restoration; distance_per_scale is millimetres
per unit drone scale. Check camera continuity, key clearance and the captured
climb after changes. The first credits.json camera matches the final rotated
assembly: elevation 21.865 degrees, azimuth -57.653 degrees (OpenSCAD X angle
68.135). Recompute it if the final camera or turntable angle changes.
